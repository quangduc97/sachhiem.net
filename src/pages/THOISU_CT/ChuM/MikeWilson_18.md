  <p><b>Comparing the plans</b> </p>
<p>Obamacare – Forbids  insurers from charging older people more than three times what they charge  younger people.</p>
<p>Trump bill – Allows  insurers to charge older consumers five times what younger consumers pay.</p>
<p>Obamacare — Prohibits  denying coverage for pre-existing conditions.</p>
<p>Trump bill — Insurers  cover pre-existing conditions, but could charge more if states allow.</p>
<p>Obamacare — Must buy  health insurance or pay penalty.</p>
<p>Trump bill — Penalties  go away, insurers can charge 30 percent more if consumer fails to maintain  continuous coverage.</p>
<p>Obamacare — Requires  “essential benefits” coverage such as maternity, preventive care, mental health  and substance abuse.</p>
<p>Trump bill — Lets states  seek waivers essential benefits rules.</p>
<p>Obamacare — Provides  subsidies to cover consumer’s costs for those making up to about $64,000.</p>
<p>Trump bill — Gives tax  credits up to $4,000 a year to those over 60, as low as $2,000 for those under  30.</p>
<p>Obamacare — Imposes  taxes on insurance and health companies, higher-income people.</p>
<p>Trump bill — Repeals tax  increases.</p>

  <p class="Tuaduoi"><b>NEW: Trump, GOP hail bill to gut  Obamacare, ‘rescue’ Americans</b></p>
  
 <p>    By <a href="https://www.mypalmbeachpost.com/staff/charles-elmore/" target="_blank">Charles Elmore</a> and <a href="https://www.mypalmbeachpost.com/staff/antonio-fins/" target="_blank">Antonio Fins</a> - Palm Beach Post Staff Writer </p>
  <p>By a razor-thin margin,  the U.S. House passed sweeping changes to the Affordable Care Act on Thursday,  handing a long-anticipated political win to <a href="https://www.palmbeachpost.com/news/donald-trump-coverage/VvztmOU9XdgNamrY7dmsSI/" target="_blank">President Donald Trump</a> even  as <i>opponents warned it would haunt the  GOP by leaving millions worse off, with older and low-income Floridians taking  some of the costliest hits in the nation.</i> </p>
<p>    Trump called it a “great  plan” and said it helps consumers because Obamacare was essentially “dead  unless we pay lots of ransom money to the insurance companies.” </p>
<p>  “This has brought the  Republican Party together,” Trump said while surrounded by GOP lawmakers in the  White House Rose Garden. “We’re going to get this finished.” </p>
<p>    Passing by just a  four-vote margin, 217-213, the American Health Care Act needed virtually every  vote it could get from Republican lawmakers such as U.S. Rep. Brian Mast,  R-Palm City. </p>
  <p><b>READ MORE: <a href="https://www.palmbeachpost.com/news/obamacare-not-death-spiral-the-case-near-trump-palm-beach-door/FH40DavWjaJH79RvEfKuiJ/" target="_blank">Obamacare ‘not in death spiral’? The  case near Trump’s Palm Beach door</a></b> </p> 
  <p>  “The American Health  Care Act delivers relief for families by ensuring that you get to choose your  coverage and the federal government can’t tax you based on what you think is  best for your family,” Mast said. “The bill returns control of health care from  Washington back to you and restores access to quality, affordable options that  are tailored to your individual needs.” </p>
<p>  <i>The  bill would let states allow insurers to charge older and sicker people more and  offer less comprehensive plans, potentially bringing down costs for healthier,  younger and higher-income people</i>. </p>
  <p><b>MORE: <a href="https://protectingyourpocket.blog.palmbeachpost.com/2017/04/25/oops-trumps-obamacare-cash-block-would-cost-taxpayers-report-says/" target="_blank">Oops, Trump’s Obamacare cash block  would cost $2.3B, report says</a></b> </p>
 <p>    <b><i>But  it hands $1 trillion in tax cuts to corporations and the wealthy and hurts  people who need care most,</i></b> opponents said. All 193 House Democrats voted against it,  joined by 20 Republicans. </p>
<p>  “Shame  on the Republicans for resurrecting this monstrosity of a health-care bill,” said U.S. Rep. Lois Frankel, D-West Palm Beach. <b><i>“It guts the crown jewel of the Affordable Care Act:  protection for the millions of Americans with a pre-existing condition</i></b><i>. These cancer survivors, children with autism,  diabetics, and pregnant women—among many others—will pay higher costs or worse,  won’t get covered at all.”</i></p>
  <p>The measure would remove  penalties on federal health insurance mandates and lessen the tax bite from the  2010 Affordable Care Act, dubbed Obamacare after former President Obama. </p>
  <p><b>RELATED: <a href="https://www.mypalmbeachpost.com/business/obamacare-explode-insurers-say-decisions-now-matter-lot/YCyoV7BEyUYY33tyoTcAwN/" target="_blank">Obamacare to ‘explode’? Insurers say  decisions now matter a lot</a></b> </p>
<p>    The measure passed  exactly six weeks after an embarrassing failure for Ryan and the Trump  administration in their pursuit of campaign promises to “repeal and replace”  Obamacare. That effort, which was supposed to have been passed on the ACA’s  seventh anniversary, was instead scuttled when the rank-and-file members of the  conservative Freedom Caucus refused to support it. </p>
<p>    This  time around, it was Republican moderates that were recalcitrant. They balked at  what they considered an erosion of protections for people with pre-existing  conditions in the reworked bill. </p>
<p>    This was especially of  interest to Florida, where on Wednesday the president of the American Medical  Association warned there are 3.1 million people with pre-existing conditions in  the state. </p>
<p>    An  amendment added to the bill providing <b><i>$8 billion over five years</i></b> to  state high-risk pools was designed to allay those concerns, and persuaded a  handful of Republicans to support the measure. </p>
<p>    Opponents  said <b><i>that was a drop in the bucket compared to what would really be  needed </i></b>for such pools, which they said<b><i>could exceed $100  billion.</i></b></p>
  <p>Senior advocacy  organization AARP warned before the vote that people with pre-existing conditions could have to pay <b><i>additional  costs of up to $25,000 or more a year</i></b> in high-risk pools, based on  past experience. </p>
<p>    In  addition, <b><i>people 50 to 64 on lower incomes could pay up to $13,000  more in premiums and deductibles</i></b> in what AARP officials call an age tax. </p>
<p>  <b><i>“These are unaffordable  amounts,”</i></b> said AARP  legislative policy director David Certner said Thursday. </p>
  <p>Florida has more people  than any other state, 454,000, between the ages of 50 to 64 who receive  subsidies in the ACA marketplace. </p>
<p>    The state also receives  the most subsidies in the country, $5.2 billion, to make premiums more  affordable for people of all ages. </p>
<p>    More than 1.5 million  Floridians get coverage on the ACA marketplace, the most of any state that uses  the federal exchange. </p>
  <p>“There  is a lot that Congress could work on to improve health care in America,” said Rep. Ted Deutch, D-Boca Raton. “Instead, Republicans forced a partisan bill  through the House that will rip away health care from 24 million people.” </p>
<p>    In many ways, the battle  is just beginning on a bill that now goes to the Senate. </p>
<p>    But it needed to happen,  GOP House Speaker Paul Ryan said before the vote, with insurers pulling out of  states including Iowa and Virginia. </p>
<p>    In Florida, Mast noted  only one insurer sold individual policies on the ACA exchange in Martin and St.  Lucie counties. </p>
<p>  “We will not falter,”  Ryan said. “We will replace. Today is the day we’re going to do this.” </p>
<p>    U.S. Rep. Doug Collins,  R-Ga., said the bill would “gut Obamacare and rescue the American people.” </p>
<p>    U.S. Rep. Tom Rooney,  R-Okeechoboee, said, “I gave my word to my constituents who lost their coverage  under Obamacare and were forced to work several part time jobs to pay for plans  on the exchanges they couldn’t afford.” </p>
<p>    The revised American  Health Care Act has not been scored by the Congressional Budget Office, but  Freedom Caucus chairman Mark Meadows, R-N.C., said it will “drive premiums  down” for many (young) people. </p>
<p>    The original plan would  have pushed 24 million out of coverage, CBO projected. Meadows said numbers in  the revised bill could look “more attractive,” though the House did not wait  for them before voting. </p>
<p>    Democratic leader Nancy  Pelosi, speaking on the House floor Thursday, said, “Forcing the vote without a CBO score shows the  Republicans are afraid of the facts.” </p>
<p>    Thursday’s  vote will be “tattooed on their foreheads", she said. </p>
