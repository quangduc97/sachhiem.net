<p>  By <a href="https://www.wsj.com/news/author/eric-sylvers" target="_blank">Eric Sylvers</a></p>
<p>Italy  presented a four-point peace plan this week to United Nations Secretary General  Antonio Guterres to end the conflict in Ukraine. </p>
<p align="center"><img src="https://pub-756b239a5ae84d4c8c8ff709f375d430.r2.dev/THOISU_CT/Hinh/UKRAINE/2022_05_20BT_NgoaigiaoY_ LuigiDiMaio.jpg" width="400" height="266" alt=""></p>
<p align="center"><i>Bộ trưởng Ngoại giao Ý Luigi Di Maio đã nói chuyện với báo chí tại Turin hôm thứ Sáu về đề xuất hòa bình của Ukraine</i>.</p>
<p>  The plan calls for local cease-fires to allow for civilian  evacuations, and creating the conditions for a general cease-fire leading  "to a long-lasting peace," Foreign Minister Luigi Di Maio said on  Friday, according to the Associated Press. </p>
<p>  Italian newspaper La Repubblica reported earlier on the  proposal, with points that mostly mirror comments made publicly by Prime  Minister Mario Draghi and other Western European leaders. </p>
<p>  <b>1. </b>The first step in the peace plan, according to the  newspaper, is a <b>cease-fire </b>and the demilitarization of the front  lines. </p>
<p>  <b>2.</b> The second point is that Ukraine would be a <b>neutral </b>country,  with its security guaranteed by a not-yet-identified group of countries. The  contours of this part of the plan would be discussed at a peace conference. </p>
<p>  <b>3.</b> The third point is a bilateral agreement between Russia  and Ukraine to clarify the future of <b>Crimea and Donbas</b>. The  agreement would deal with cultural and language rights and guarantee the free  movement of people, goods and capital. The plan, according to Repubblica,  indicates that Crimea and Donbas would have <b>almost complete autonomy,</b> including  in questions of defense, but would be <b>part of Ukraine.</b> </p>
<p>  <b>4.</b> The fourth point would be a multilateral peace agreement  between the European Union and Russia that would include a staged <b>withdrawal  of Russian troops </b>from Ukraine and the<b> winding down of Western  sanctions</b> against Moscow. </p>
