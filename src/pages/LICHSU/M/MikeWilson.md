<p><b>Iran aims high ahead of push for final nuclear deal</b></p>
<p>
<a href="https://www.ap.org/"><img border="0" width="23" height="28" src="https://pub-756b239a5ae84d4c8c8ff709f375d430.r2.dev/THOISU_CT/Hinh/ISIS/clip_image001.png" alt="Associated Press"></a> </p>
<p>
<i>By NASSER KARIMI and ADAM SCHRECK</i></p>
  <p><i>...</i> </p>
<p> Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali  Khamenei will have the final say on whether Iran agrees to a deal that could  transform its relationship with the wider world -- and he is keeping everyone  guessing. </p>
<p> In his first comments on last week's  deal, Khamenei told a gathering of religious poets on Thursday that he "is  neither for nor against" it. <i>His reasoning was matter-of-fact</i>:  Because the agreement was only the framework of a final deal and not the accord  itself, "<i>nothing has been done yet</i>," he said. </p>
<p>
  "<i>What has happened so far neither guarantees a deal... <b>nor does it  guarantee the content </b>of a deal</i>," he said. "<i><b>It doesn't even guarantee the talks will go on until  the end </b> and will lead  to a deal</i>." </p>
<p> Khamenei did say, however, that the  punitive "<b><i>sanctions should be  lifted completely, on the very day of the deal</i></b>" —  something that has not been agreed upon. </p>
<p>He cautioned that  the six world powers — the five permanent U.N. Security Council members and  Germany — <b><i>are "not to be trusted"</i></b> and may try "to  limit Iran" in further talks. </p>
<p> And he urged Iranian negotiators not  to accept any "unconventional inspections" of Iran's nuclear  facilities, stressing that <i>the  inspection of military facilities would not be permitted. (<b>as they did in  Iraq, just before the invasion by George W.  Bush</b> . nth-fl)</i> </p>
<p> At the same time, however, he said a  successful deal would show that negotiations are possible on other issues  beyond the nuclear program. </p>
<p>
    Khamenei has backed the negotiating  team despite criticism of the process from hard-liners. And this week, the  negotiators won a major endorsement from the chief of Iran's Revolutionary  Guard, Gen. Mohammad Ali Jafari, who praised their work "<i>in  defending the rights of the Iranian nation</i>." (to have nuclear  research and development <i>for peaceful purposes</i> . nth-fl) </p>
