							<img border="0" src="https://pub-756b239a5ae84d4c8c8ff709f375d430.r2.dev/SACHNGOAI/Hinhanh/ChurchSexabuse.jpg" width="203" height="152" align="left" hspace="10">A secret Vatican document, outlining procedures 
							for handling allegations of sex abuse by priests, 
							has been published in the US and UK. Lawyers acting 
							for alleged victims of abuse say the document proves 
							that for decades the Vatican has systematically 
							obstructed the course of justice in order to protect 
							Catholic priests. </p>
							<p>Church lawyers, on the other hand, argue that the 
							document referred only to church law and did not 
							order bishops to engage in criminal cover-ups. </p>
							<p>But the US lawyer who uncovered the document told 
							a British newspaper the archive material is a 
							smoking gun. </p>
							<p>The lawyer, Daniel Shea, told The Observer 
							newspaper the 1962 Vatican instruction was "a 
							devious attempt to conceal criminal conduct". </p>
							<p>It "is a blueprint for deception and 
							concealment", the Sunday newspaper quotes him as 
							saying.</p>
<p>The document was sent to every bishop in the world, with orders to keep it 
under lock and key, the BBC's David Willey in Rome reports. </p>
<p>Mr Shea, who represents alleged victims of abuse by Catholic priests, 
obtained the document from an American priest and handed it over to US 
authorities. </p>
<p>It bears the seal of Pope John XXIII, and calls for "strictest" secrecy in 
dealing with abuse allegations within the Church. </p>
<p>It threatens to expel from the Church anyone who breaks their silence. </p>
<p>It deals primarily with priests accused of propositioning congregants during 
confession, the Catholic rite in which the faithful tell priests of their sins. 
Mr Shea says he was given the document by a clergyman in Germany who has close 
dealings with the Vatican. </p>
<p>He handed a copy of the document to US authorities late last month. </p>
<p class="TuaDoLeft">Church's defence </p>
<p>Early in August, the US Conference of Catholic Bishops said that the 
document, "Instruction on the manner of proceeding in cases of solicitation", 
had not been in force for years.</p>
<p>The conference said it was superseded by later guidelines introduced in the 
1960s, 1970s and in 1983, the Associated Press reported. </p>
<p>The Vatican's 1983 Code of Canon Law says a priest found to have abused a 
minor can be defrocked. </p>
<p>The conference also said the 1962 document "had no bearing on civil law" and 
had been misrepresented. </p>
<p>The US Catholic Church has been rocked by revelations of sexual abuse by 
priests. </p>
<p>Bishops there have been accused of protecting priests suspected of sexual 
abuse, moving them to new parishes rather than confronting the problem. </p>
<p>The leader of England and Wales's Catholics, Archbishop Cormac 
Murphy-O'Connor, has apologised for relocating priest Michael Hill when 
accusations against him arose. 
<img border="0" src="https://pub-756b239a5ae84d4c8c8ff709f375d430.r2.dev/SACHNGOAI/Hinhanh/PEOPLE/CardinalLaw1.jpg" width="203" height="152" align="right" hspace="10"></p>
<p>Vatican critics say the 1962 document suggests bishops who moved priests may 
have been acting in line with instructions from Rome. </p>
<p>They also say its age refutes Church claims that the problem of sex abuse by 
priests is a new one. </p>
<p>The Archbishop of Boston, Cardinal Bernard Law, was forced to resign late 
last year after he admitted he had covered up sexual abuse by priests for many 
years. </p>
<p>The Church faces hundreds of civil lawsuits by people who say they were 
abused.</p>
