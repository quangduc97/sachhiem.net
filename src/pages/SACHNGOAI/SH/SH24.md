     <p class="Tuaduoi">Report details sexual abuse by more than 300 priests in Pennsylvania's Catholic Church </p>
  <p class="TacgiaL">By Daniel Burke and Susannah Cullinane
</p>
  <p> CNN</p>
     <p><b>(CNN)</b>A new grand jury  report says that internal documents from six Catholic dioceses in Pennsylvania  show that more than 300 "predator priests" have been credibly accused  of sexually abusing more than 1,000 child victims.<br>
"We believe that the real number of children whose records  were lost or who were afraid ever to come forward is in the thousands,"  the grand jury report says. <br>
"Priests were raping little boys and girls, and the men of  God who were responsible for them not only did nothing; they hid it all. For  decades. Monsignors, auxiliary bishops, bishops, archbishops, cardinals have  mostly been protected; many, including some named in this report, have been promoted."<br>
The grand jury described the church's methods as "a  playbook for concealing the truth" after FBI agents identified a series of  practices they found in diocese files.<br>
The&nbsp;<a href="https://media-downloads.pacourts.us/InterimRedactedReportandResponses.pdf?cb=22148" target="_blank">lengthy report</a>, released Tuesday afternoon,  investigates clergy sexual abuse dating to 1947 in six dioceses: Allentown,  Erie, Greensburg, Harrisburg, Pittsburgh and Scranton.<br>
Pennsylvania's  two other dioceses, Philadelphia and Altoona-Johnstown, have been the subjects  of earlier grand jury reports, which found similarly damaging information about  clergy and bishops in those dioceses.</p>
     <p>"There have been other reports about  child sex abuse within the Catholic Church. But never on this scale," the  grand jurors wrote in Tuesday's report.<br>
"For many of us, those earlier stories  happened someplace else, someplace away. Now we know the truth: it happened  everywhere."<br>
The grand jurors said that "almost every  instance of abuse we found is too old to be prosecuted." But charges have  been filed against two priests, one in Erie diocese and another in Greensburg  diocese, who have been accused of abusing minors.<br>
"We learned of these abusers directly  from their dioceses -- which we hope is a sign that the church is finally  changing its ways," the grand jurors said. "And there may be more  indictments in the future; investigation continues."<br>
At a news conference announcing the report's  release, Pennsylvania Attorney General Josh Shapiro called it the  "largest, most comprehensive report into child sexual abuse within the  Catholic Church ever produced in the United States."</p>
     <h3><b>Molestations and rapes</b></h3>
     <p>At times, the lengthy catalog of clergy sexual abuses in the  report is difficult to read. As the grand jurors note, priests and other  Catholic leaders victimized boys and girls, teens and pre-pubescent children.<br>
       Some victims were plied with alcohol and groped or molested, the  report says. Others were orally, vaginally or anally raped, according to the  grand jurors.<br>
  "But all of them were brushed aside, in every part of the  state, by church leaders who preferred to protect the abusers and their  institution above all."<br>
       Among the more egregious cases, the grand jury reports that:<br>
       • In the Greensburg diocese, a priest impregnated a 17-year-old,  forged a pastor's signature on a marriage certificate and divorced the girl  months later. According to the grand jury, the priest was allowed to stay in  ministry by finding a "benevolent bishop."<br>
       • Another priest in Greensburg groomed middle-school students  for sex, according to the grand jury, by telling them that Mary had to  "bite off the cord" and "lick" Jesus clean after the  Nativity.</p>
<p>• In Harrisburg, a priest abused five sisters from the same  family and collected samples of their urine, pubic hair and menstrual blood.<br>
• Also in Harrisburg, a priest raped a 7-year-old girl who was  in the hospital after her tonsils were removed, according to the report.<br>
• In Pittsburgh, church officials said that a 15-year-old boy  "pursued" and "literally seduced" a priest. A church report  later acknowledged that the priest had admitted to "sado-masochistic"  activities with several boys.<br>
• In the Allentown diocese, a priest admitted sexually molesting  a boy and pleaded for help, according to documents, but was left in ministry  for several more years.<br>
• Also in Allentown, a priest who had abused several boys,  according to the grand jury, was given a recommendation to work at Disney  World.<br>
• In Scranton, a priest who later served prison time for abusing  children was found to have been HIV-positive for years.<br>
Tuesday's news conference began with a short video of three  victims who told how they were abused and how it changed their lives.<br>
An 83-year-old man said he couldn't show any affection to his  wife and children as a result of the abuse he suffered. A woman said the abuse  started when she was 18 months old. Another man said, "When you have the  priest touching you every day, that's a hard memory to have. The first erection  that you have is at the hands of a priest."<br>
The victims said this was "not a vendetta against the  church" and that abusers have "to be accountable in the church for  what they did."</p>
<h3><b>'Grave failings'</b></h3>
<p>The grand jury's searing report comes as the  Catholic Church, including Pope Francis, is struggling to contain a sexual  abuse scandal rapidly consuming the church on several continents.<br>
In Australia, a bishop has been found guilty  of covering up sexual abuse. In Chile, the Pope was forced to recant his  dismissal of an abuse scandal involving a prominent priest and bishops accused  of covering up his crimes.</p>
<p>And in the United States, a prominent  archbishop was removed from the powerful College of Cardinals following reports  that he had molested a teenage altar boy and several others while he was rising  through the church's ranks. Meanwhile, bishops in Boston and Nebraska are  investigating possible cases of sexual abuse in Catholic seminaries.<br>
  "The report of the Pennsylvania grand  jury again illustrates the pain of those who have been victims of the crime of  sexual abuse by individual members of our clergy, and by those who shielded  abusers and so facilitated an evil that continued for years or even  decades," Cardinal Daniel DiNardo, president of the U.S. Conference of  Catholic Bishops and Bishop Timothy L. Doherty, chair of the bishops' Committee  for the Protection of Children and Young People, said in a statement.<br>
  "As a body of bishops, we are shamed by  and sorry for the sins and omissions by Catholic priests and Catholic  bishops."<br>
  The Vatican declined to comment on the grand  jury report.<br>
  DiNardo and Doherty noted that the grand  jury's report spans 70 years, and many of the abuse accusations were made  before 2002, when the bishops adopted new policies. The policies, known as the  Dallas Charter, after the city in which they were adopted, have been revised in  2011 and 2018.<br>
  The charter, the bishops said, "commits  us to respond promptly and compassionately to victims, report the abuse of  minors, remove offenders and take ongoing action to prevent abuse."<br>
  For weeks, many Catholics in the United States  had been warily waiting for the Pennsylvania grand jury's report, especially as  bishops in the state began publicly releasing the names of accused clergy in an  apparent attempt to preempt some of the report's findings.<br>
  In a statement on Monday before the report was  published, Cardinal Donald Wuerl, the former Bishop of Pittsburgh who now heads  the Archdiocese of Washington, said the report "will be a reminder of the  grave failings that the church must acknowledge and for which it must seek  forgiveness."<br>
  "We are now in the midst of a new era  where our communal bonds of trust are once again being tested by the sin of  abuse."</p>
<h3><b>Delays in publication</b></h3>
<p>Court action had delayed the report's publication. A number of  individuals named in the report claimed that its findings were false or misleading,  that they were denied due process of law and that its release would impair  their reputations.<br>
  On July 27, the Pennsylvania Supreme Court&nbsp;<a href="https://www.pacourts.us/assets/files/setting-6283/file-7211.pdf?cb=b95bb0" target="_blank">ordered the  grand jury report&nbsp;</a>to be released by 2 p.m. August 14 with  redactions in sections where litigation was ongoing.<br>
  <a href="https://pub-756b239a5ae84d4c8c8ff709f375d430.r2.devhttps://www.attorneygeneral.gov/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/2018-07-25-Letter-for-His-Holiness-Pope-Francis.pdf" target="_blank">State Attorney  General Josh Shapiro had written to Pope Francis</a>&nbsp;on July 25,  requesting that the Pontiff direct church leaders to stop "efforts to  silence the survivors."<br>
  "A comprehensive investigation by the Office of Attorney  General found widespread sexual abuse of children and a systematic coverup by  leaders of the Catholic Church," Shapiro said in his letter.</p>
<h3><b>Harrisburg abuse list</b></h3>
<p>On August  1, the leader of one of the largest Roman Catholic dioceses in Pennsylvania<a href="https://pub-756b239a5ae84d4c8c8ff709f375d430.r2.devhttps://www.youthprotectionhbg.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/Diocese-of-Harrisburg-List-of-Clergy-and-Seminarians-Accused-of-Sexual-Abuse-of-a-Child.pdf" target="_blank">&nbsp;released a  list&nbsp;</a>identifying 71 priests, deacons and seminarians accused of  "substantiated" sexual misconduct over the past seven decades.<br>
  Bishop  Ronald Gainer<a href="https://pub-756b239a5ae84d4c8c8ff709f375d430.r2.devhttps://www.youthprotectionhbg.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/Diocese-of-Harrisburg-List-of-Clergy-and-Seminarians-Accused-of-Sexual-Abuse-of-a-Child.pdf" target="_blank">&nbsp;</a>of  the Harrisburg Diocese also&nbsp;<a href="https://pub-756b239a5ae84d4c8c8ff709f375d430.r2.devhttps://www.youthprotectionhbg.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/Diocese-of-Harrisburg-List-of-Clergy-and-Seminarians-Accused-of-Sexual-Abuse-of-a-Child.pdf" target="_blank">issued an  apology</a>&nbsp;on behalf of the religious community.<br>
  "That  conduct has left a legacy of pain and sorrow that is still being felt," he  wrote. "I apologize for these actions."<br>
  While most  men on the Harrisburg list are accused of sexually abusing children, others  were investigated for inappropriate behavior, such as kissing or inappropriately  communicating with a minor, Gainer wrote. Others were accused of viewing or  possessing child pornography.<br>
  The list  did not say how the diocese handled most of the accusations and did not give  the men's current whereabouts, though a few cases that were forwarded to civil  authorities were more detailed.<br>
  <i>CNN's  Carma Hassan, Julia Jones, Madeleine Thompson, Augusta Anthony and Janet  DiGiacomo contributed to this report.</i></p>
