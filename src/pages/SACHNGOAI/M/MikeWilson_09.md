<p><a href="https://www.msn.com/en-us/news/politics/mitch-mcconnell-s-historic-miscalculation/ar-AAZZjl2" class="TuaDenLeft">Mitch McConnell’s historic miscalculation</a> </p>
<p>   <i>Opinion</i> by John Kenneth  White, Opinion Contributor - Tuesday (26/7/2022)</p>
<p>   At the conclusion of the January 6  select committee's summer hearings, Rep. Adam Kinzinger (R-Ill.) admonished his  fellow Republicans supporting former President Trump, saying, "Oaths  matter more than party tribalism or the cheap thrill of scoring political points." </p>
<p align="center"><img src="https://pub-756b239a5ae84d4c8c8ff709f375d430.r2.dev/THOISU_CT/Hinh/HOAKY/TRUMPIST/MitchMcConnell_Trump.PNG" width="400" height="232" alt=""></p>
 <p>But one Republican who repeatedly ignored that message during  the Trump years is Senate Minority Leader Mitch McConnell (R-Ky.) After  opposing Trump's conviction following his impeachment by the House in 2021,  McConnell took to the Senate floor and attempted to cast aside his not guilty  vote by excoriating Trump: "January  6 was a disgrace. American <i>citizens attacked their own  government. They used terrorism to try to stop  a specific piece of domestic business they did not like. . .  There is no  question, none, that President Trump is practically and morally responsible for  provoking the events of the day</i>." </p>
 <p>McConnell's  words were just that — words. Instead of convicting Trump, which would have  forever disqualified him from holding "any Office of honor,  Trust, or Profit under the United States," <i>McConnell let Trump off on a technicality that a  former president could not be impeached and convicted.</i> McConnell also gave Trump other  off-ramps, including lobbying against a bipartisan commission to investigate  the Capitol riot. </p>
<p>   Today,  McConnell says of the Jan. 6 hearings, "It will be interesting  to reveal all the participants that are involved," but then  immediately changes the subject to other issues he wants to discuss —  inflation, crime, immigration and the Southern border. </p>
 
 <p>After  Jan. 6, an "exhilarated" McConnell concluded that Trump was a spent  force who had "<i>totally discredited himself</i>." This was an  error of historic proportions. Today, Trump is the country's most powerful  Republican and the odds-on-favorite to be the party's 2024 presidential  nominee. Trump's imprimatur is a powerhouse in Republican contests. His  successes include a Trump acolyte winning the Republican gubernatorial  nomination in Maryland over Trump's Republican nemesis Gov. Larry Hogan; Rep.  Liz Cheney's (R-Wyo.) likely defeat at the hands of a Trump-endorsed candidate;  and winning Senate nominations for Hershel Walker, J. D. Vance and Mehmet Oz. Sixty percent of Republicans want their party to follow  Trump's leadership, and 55 percent of Republicans have an unfavorable opinion of  McConnell — thanks, in part, to Trump's calling him a "disloyal sleazebag" and an "old, broken down crow."</p>
 
 <p><i>Mitch  McConnell's sole focus is power - how to seize it and use it for partisan ends.</i> After the Republican  Party's 2010 landslide victories, McConnell  announced that <i>his top priority was  to make Barack Obama a one-term president.</i></p>
 <p>When a Supreme Court  seat became available in February 2016 after the death of Justice Antonin  Scalia, <i>McConnell kept it in  abeyance for a year (nth : denying Obama his chance to nominate a liberal  Supreme Court Justice) so Trump could name his successor. And when Justice Ruth  Bader Ginsburg died in September 2020, McConnell rushed the confirmation of Amy  Coney Barrett, giving conservatives a 6-3 supermajority</i>. </p>
<p>   While McConnell would like to be remembered for transforming the  federal judiciary, <i>his enduring legacy  will be the damage he has done to the institutions of government</i>.  For example, public approval of the (Supreme) court has fallen to 38 percent. By contrast, the court's approval numbers in  September 2020, just before McConnell's Supreme Court packing, stood at a  healthy 66 percent. </p>
 <p>We have learned  that <i>Donald Trump's dereliction of  duty and support for overturning the 2020 presidential election represents the  worst betrayal of a president's constitutional oath since James  Buchanan allowed the Southern states to secede in 1861</i>. Instead of  defending Congress's institutional prerogatives, McConnell put partisanship  above country while cynically hoping that Trump's actions on Jan. 6, 2021,  would ensure his fall from grace. </p>
<p>   Instead (nth: contrary  to McConnell's miscalculations), Trump's  unprecedented dominance of the Republican Party makes it an ongoing danger to our  constitutional republic. As Liz Cheney told a  captive audience at the Reagan Library, "<i>We are confronting a domestic threat</i> (nth: from Ttrump and his  partisan militant cult-like base)<i> that we  have never faced before. And that is a  former president who is threatening to unravel the foundations of our  constitutional republic. And he is aided by Republican leaders and elected officials who made  themselves willing hostages to this dangerous and  irrational man.</i>" </p>
<p>   ...  </p>
<p>   <i> Mitch  McConnell will be remembered as the man who helped destroy the  Senate.</i> When McConnell decided in 2021 to absolve Trump, he should  have heeded Niccolo Machiavelli's purported advice, <i>"If  you're going to shoot the king, don't miss."</i></p>
