<p class="Tuaduoi">Why Trump will be  remembered as 'the worst president America has ever had'</p>

<p><a href="https://www.alternet.org/2021/01/trump-worst-president/" target="_blank" class="nguon">https://www.alternet.org/2021/01/trump-worst-president/</a></p>

<p>Tim  Naftali, a history professor at New York University, looks back on Trump's  presidency in an article published by The Atlantic the  day before Biden's inauguration — and lays out some reasons why he considers  Trump the worst president in U.S. history. </p>
<p align="center"><img src="https://pub-756b239a5ae84d4c8c8ff709f375d430.r2.dev/THOISU_CT/Hinh/HOAKY/TRUMPIST/Trump2011.JPG" width="400" height="247" alt=""></p>

  <p>
  Three particular        failures secure Trump’s status as the worst chief executive ever to hold        the office.</p> 
<p>Saying that Trump was  worse than all 44 of the presidents who came before him is saying a lot, but Naftali  offers a lot of history to back up his argument. </p>
<p>
  "<i>As  his four years in office draw to an end," </i>Naftali writes, "<i>there's  only one title to which he can lay claim: Donald Trump is the worst president America has ever  had.</i>" </p>
<p>
  The  NYU professor goes on to say that a U.S. president serves as <i>"head of  state, head of government and commander in chief</i>" and promises to  "<i>preserve, protect and defend the Constitution of the United States</i>." </p>
<p>
  "<i>Trump was a serial violator of his oath — as evidenced by his continual use of his  office for personal financial gain — but focusing on three crucial ways in  which he betrayed it helps clarify his singular historical status</i>,"  Naftali explains. <i>"First, he failed to put the  national security interests of the United States ahead of his own political  needs. Second, in the face of a devastating pandemic, he was  grossly derelict, unable or unwilling to marshal the requisite resources to  save lives while actively encouraging public behavior that spread the disease.  And third, held to account by voters  for his failures, he refused to concede defeat and instead instigated an insurrection, stirring a mob that stormed  the Capitol</i>." </p>
<p>
  Naftali  notes that some historians have slammed Warren G. Harding as the worst  president in U.S. history. Others hold Franklin Pierce, Andrew Johnson and  James Buchanan in low regard. </p>
<p>
  "<i>Does Trump have  any modern competitors for the title of worst president?,</i>" Naftali writes.  "<i>Like Harding, a number of presidents were poor executors of the office.  President Woodrow Wilson was an awful man who  presided over an apartheid system in the nation's capital, largely confined his  support for democracy abroad to White nations, and then mishandled a (flu)  pandemic. President Herbert Hoover helped drive the U.S.  economy into the ground during the Great Depression, because the  economics he learned as a young man proved fundamentally wrong.</i>" </p>
<p>
  Naftali  continues, <i>"President George W. Bush's impulse after 9/11 to  weaken American civil liberties in the name of protecting them, and his blanket approval of interrogation  techniques universally considered torture, left Americans  disillusioned and impeded the struggle to deradicalize Islamists (he also invaded Iraq on false, unproven  pretext)…. And then there's  Richard Nixon. Before Trump, Nixon set the standard for  modern presidential failure as the first president forced from office, who  resigned ahead of impeachment. And in many ways, their presidencies have been eerily parallel.  But the comparison to Nixon reveals the ways in which Trump's presidency has  been not merely bad, but the very worst we have ever seen</i>." </p>
<p>
  Trump's  many failures, Naftali adds, range from his "<i>serial subservience to foreign strongmen such as Recep Tayyip Erdoğan of Turkey,  Kim Jong Un of North Korea, and, of course, Russia's Vladimir Putin</i>" to his "<i>effort to squeeze the Ukrainians to  get dirt on  his likely opponent in 2020, the cause of his first impeachment</i>" to his disastrous response to the coronavirus pandemic. </p>
<p>
  Naftali  observes, "<i>The COVID-19 pandemic… will have killed at least 400,000  Americans by the time he leaves office. In his inaugural address, Trump vowed an end to 'American carnage,' but in  office, he presided over needless death and suffering. Trump's failure to anticipate and then respond  to the pandemic has no equivalent in Nixon's tenure; when Nixon wasn't plotting  political subversion and revenge against his perceived enemies, he could be a  good administrator.</i>" </p>
<p>
  Trump's  presidency, Naftali laments, is ending  with "<i>his role as the chief instigator of the  attempted insurrection of January 6,</i>" which went "<i>far  beyond</i>" Nixon and the Watergate scandal. </p>
<p>
  "<i>If Trump is now  the worst president we have ever had</i>," Naftali writes, "<i>it's up to every American to ensure that  no future chief executive ever exceeds him.</i>"  (and his evil  acts)</p>
