 <h1> Những nhà hoạt động Hồng Kông tìm sự ủng hộ của Hoa Kỳ cho các cuộc biểu tình ủng hộ dân chủ</h1>

<p>(<a href="https://www.reuters.com/article/us-hongkong-protests-wong/hong-kong-activist-joshua-wong-seeks-u-s-support-for-protests-idUSKBN1VZ0LQ">Reuters, 14 Sept 2019</a>)</p>
<p>NEW YORK (Reuters) - Nhà hoạt động dân chủ Hồng Kông Joshua Wong cho biết hôm thứ Bảy (14 Sept 2019), đang tìm kiếm sự hỗ trợ của các nhà lập pháp Hoa Kỳ ủng hộ các yêu cầu của những đồng bào biểu tình của ông, những người đã dẫn đầu nhiều cuộc biểu tình trên đường phố cả tháng, bao gồm sự ủng hộ cho lời kêu gọi bầu cử tự do. </p>
<p>Hồng Kông được Anh trả lại cho Trung Quốc năm 1997 dưới thỏa ước ký kết là một quốc gia hai chế độ, để bảo đảm dân Hồng Kông được hưởng tự do mà người dân lục địa không có.</p>
<p> Wong, người thanh niên dẫn đầu cuộc biểu tình "che dù" ở Hồng Kông năm 2014, nói với phóng viên báo Reuters ở Nứu Ước trước chuyến viếng thăm Washington. Các cuộc biểu tình sau này không có lãnh đạo, bắt đầu từ việc chống "dự luật dẫn độ" nhưng dần dần trở thành đòi dân chủ và độc lập khỏi Trung Quốc lục địa. </p>
<p> Cuộc b---- The latest protests, often involving violent clashes with police, have roiled Hong Kong for more than three months. Millions of people have taken to the city’s streets, even shutting down its airport for two days. Demonstrators’ demands include an independent inquiry into what they describe as police brutality and universal suffrage.</p>
<p>“We hope ... for bipartisan support,” Wong said of his trip to Washington, adding that U.S. lawmakers should demand the inclusion of a human rights clause in ongoing U.S.-China trade negotiations.</p>
<p>He said he also hoped to convince members of Congress to pass the Hong Kong Human Rights and Democracy Act, which would require an annual justification of the special treatment that for decades has been afforded to the Chinese-ruled city by Washington, including trade and business privileges.</p>

<p>China has accused foreign powers, especially the United States and Britain, of fueling the unrest.</p>

<p>China is eager to quell the protests before the 70th anniversary on Oct. 1 of the founding of the People’s Republic of China. The nation has not involved its military in efforts to quash the protests.</p>
<p>Wong said the Hong Kong people would keep fighting for their cause through the anniversary.</p>
<p>“We will continue our protest with our course on free elections,” he said. “I see no reason for us to give up and it’s time for the world to stand with Hong Kong.”</p>

<p>Pro-democracy student activist Joshua Wong testifies before the Congressional-Executive Committee on China about the recent protests in Hong Kong. In his opening statement, Wong warns lawmakers about Beijing's intentions regarding the city once ruled by Britain. "Today, we are approaching dangerously close to one country, one system. The present state of affairs reflect Beijing's inability to understand, let alone govern, a free society," Wong says. Wong, along with other activists, is in Washington to lobby Congress to pass the Hong Kong Human Rights and Democracy Act of 2019, a bill that would sanction individuals who suppress basic freedoms in Hong Kong.</p>

<p><b>Swedish activist Greta Thunberg takes climate protest to Trump</b></p>
<p>(Aljazeera: 13 Sept 2019) -
 After bringing her message from Stockholm to Berlin, Paris, Rome and New York, the teen advocate hits Washington, too.</p>

<p align="center"> <img src="https://pub-756b239a5ae84d4c8c8ff709f375d430.r2.dev/THOISU_CT/Hinh/HOAKY/GretaThunberg_16t_WADC_13Sep2019.jpg" width="400" height="225" alt=""></p>
<p align="center">Teen climate activist Greta Thunberg and other environmental advocates join Washington, DC-area students at a rally on the Ellipse near the White House [Leah Millis/Reuters]</p>
<p>Swedish teen activist Greta Thunberg, who has shot to global fame for inspiring worldwide student strikes to promote an urgent response to&nbsp;climate change, took her mission to&nbsp;United States&nbsp;President&nbsp;Donald Trump’s doorstep on Friday with a protest in Washington, DC.</p>
<p>Hundreds of mostly young people gathered across the street from the White House to meet her carrying signs that read "People or Profit?" and "Warming!", chanting "This is a crisis, act like it!" and "Business as usual is not enough".</p>
<p>Jennifer Morash, a doctoral candidate in plant science, brought her daughter Adeline, aged nine, to the rally after getting permission from her school in neighbouring Maryland. "I just want to make sure we all have a happy future - and for people to take climate change seriously," Adeline said.</p>
<p>Many protesters laid down and kept still for 11 minutes for a die-in representing the 11 years that scientists believe the world has to make the changes needed to stave off an even more dangerous climate crisis and avoid a dramatic&nbsp;increase in global temperatures.</p>
<p>The demonstration marked the first high-profile event of Thunberg's six-day visit to the US capital, intended to pressure the Trump administration ahead of the September 23 United Nations climate summit, where world leaders will be asked to ramp up their carbon-cutting ambitions to fend off global warming.</p>
<h2>'Guru of the apocalypse'</h2>
<p>Trump is among a small minority of global leaders to openly question the science of climate change.</p>
<p>He has announced his intention to withdraw the US from the&nbsp;Paris Agreement&nbsp;– a worldwide pact to stem the rise in global temperatures - and has a policy of maximising US production of&nbsp;fossil fuels.</p>
<p>Thunberg has said she does not believe she can convince Trump or other climate science deniers that global warming is real, but hopes they will take briefings from "actual scientists and experts in this area".</p>
