  <p>BY&nbsp;<span itemtype="https://schema.org/Person" itemprop="author" itemscope=""><a href="https://www.newsweek.com/authors/conor-gaffey-0" rel="author" itemprop="url"><span itemprop="name">CONOR GAFFEY</span></a></span>&nbsp; ON 6/12/17 AT 5:47 AM</p>
  <p>&nbsp;Pope Francis has given an  ultimatum to unruly Nigerian priests: Pledge “total obedience” to him and their  bishop within a month, or lose their jobs.</p>
<p>    Francis met with a delegation from the diocese of Ahiara in  southern Nigeria at the Vatican and delivered the message Saturday, according  to&nbsp;<a href="https://en.radiovaticana.va/news/2017/06/10/pope_receives_delegation_from_nigerian_diocese_of_ahiara/1318206" target="_blank">Vatican Radio</a>.</p>
<p>    Since 2012, the Catholic church has faced problems in Ahiara, as  the majority of clergy have refused to accept Bishop Peter Okpaleke, who was  appointed&nbsp;<a href="https://www.newsweek.com/after-ratzinger-enough-old-european-popes-63353" target="_blank">under Francis’ predecessor Pope Benedict XVI</a>, as their leader.</p>
<p>  <strong> </strong>The standoff is related to the fact that Okpaleke does not hail  from the local Mbaise ethnic group and priests of the diocese view him as an  outsider. The clergy protested his appointment in 2012, with some demonstrators  locking the doors of the cathedral to stop the new bishop from entering, according  to Catholic news site&nbsp;<a href="https://cruxnow.com/analysis/2017/06/11/three-ironies-dramatic-show-papal-muscle-nigeria/" target="_blank">Crux</a>.</p>
<p>    In the frank meeting with the Nigerian delegation, Francis  demanded that every priest of the Ahiara diocese must individually write him a  letter by July 9, in which each one must “clearly manifest total obedience to  the pope and…be willing to accept the bishop whom the pope sends and has  appointed.”</p>
<p>    Any priest who fails to write such a letter will be suspended  from carrying out his duties—including the celebration of mass and other  sacraments, such as baptism—and will “lose his current office,” according to  the pope’s address.</p>
<p><a href="https://www.newsweek.com/nigerias-megacity-lagos-shuts-70-churches-and-20-mosques-dim-din-476489" target="_blank">Nigeria is roughly equally split</a>&nbsp;between Christians, who  mainly live in the south, and Muslims in the north. The Ahiara diocese served  over half a million Catholics in 2015, and had 128 priests attached to the diocese,  plus seven other priests,&nbsp;<a href="https://abcnews.go.com/International/wireStory/pope-nigerian-priests-fired-obey-47969025" target="_blank">the AP reported</a>.</p>
<p>  The pope acknowledged in his address that his ultimatum “seems  very hard” but said that drastic action was needed because the crisis meant  that “the people of God are scandalized.” Francis also dismissed “tribalism” as  a reason for the conflict and said that the rebellion was “an attempted taking  of the vineyard of the Lord.”</p>

<p>  Francis said that he  had even considered suppressing the diocese—an&nbsp;<a href="https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05001a.htm" target="_blank">extreme measure</a>&nbsp;that usually only occurs  when a Catholic population has been dispersed by persecution—but that he had  decided not to do so, as it would inflict suffering on the region’s Catholics.</p>
<p>  He described the  church in Ahiara as “like a widow for having prevented the bishop from coming  to the diocese” and said that the pope “cannot be indifferent” to the  situation. “Whoever was opposed to Bishop Okpaleke taking possession of the  diocese wants to destroy the Church,” said Francis, suggesting that some of the  rebellious priests may have been manipulated from outside the diocese or from  abroad.</p>
<p>  <a href="https://www.newsweek.com/jesus-has-found-home-here-rise-catholicism-africa-399114" target="_blank">The Catholic Church has grown  hugely in</a>&nbsp;Sub-Saharan Africa, and Francis has promoted several  African bishops to the rank of cardinal since becoming pope in 2013.</p>
